    <p><strong>Bilingual Emotional Processing</strong>. My dissertation examines emotional processing in bilinguals while they watch disgusting narrative videos as measured by self-report and electrophysical measures of autonomic arousal (Electodermal activity/skin conductance). Additionally, this project explores how the emotional qualities and task language of the videos influence incidental vocabulary learning outcomes. This project aims to provide better understanding of how bilinguals process emotional language in more naturalistic situations (e.g., beyond single words and phrases) as well as provide practical implications to vocabulary learning in classroom settings.</p>
    <p><strong>Language Usage in Heritage Speakers</strong>. This work examines linguistic features of spontaneous Spanish speech from Heritage speaker bilinguals in Chicago. It aims to reproduce Kyle and Eguchi (2023)'s methodological approach while additionally incorporating sociolinguistic factors known to influence to heritage language language use (generation, Spanish variety, age, age of arrival).</p>
