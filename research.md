    <details>
      <summary><strong>Bilingual Emotional Processing</strong></summary>

      My dissertation examines emotional processing in bilinguals while they watch disgusting narrative videos as measured by self-report and electrophysical measures of autonomic arousal (Electodermal activity/skin conductance). Additionally, this project explores how the emotional quality and task language of the videos influence incidental vocabulary learning outcomes. This project aims to provide better understanding of how bilinguals process emotional language in more naturalistic situations (e.g., beyond single words and phrases) as well as provide practical implications to vocabulary learning in classroom settings.
    </details>

    <br>

    <details>
      <summary><strong>Language Usage in Heritage Speakers</strong></summary>

      This work examines linguistic features of spontaneous Spanish speech from Heritage speaker bilinguals in Chicago. It aims to reproduce Kyle and Eguchi (2023)'s methodological approach while additionally incorporating sociolinguistic factors known to influence to heritage language language use (generation, Spanish variety, age, age of arrival).
    </details>

    <br>

    <details> 
      <summary><strong>Statistical Word Learning</strong></summary>

      This work explores Mandarin Chinese word acquisition within cross situational word learning (CSWL) paradigms. Via web-based psycholinguistic experiments, participants associate sounds (Mandarin words: 🔊 “橙子”) with images (word referents: [🍊]) by tracking statistical patterns by which both stimuli cooccur. 
      
      <ul>
        <li>The first experiment <a href="https://natecook9.github.io/CSWL_Study" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Click here</a> explores the role of emotional primes in Mandarin statistical learning, specifically if seeing emotional facial expressions increases accuracy and reduces response times across trial as well as if specific emotional facial expressions (disgust, fear, happy) hold particular influence on statistical learning outcomes.</li>
        <li>The second experiment <a href="https://natecook9.github.io/CSWL_Study2" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Click here</a> examines if statistical learning is sensitive to the auditory input: are word associations learned equally when presented with machine- and human-produced speech?</li>
      </ul>
    </details>
